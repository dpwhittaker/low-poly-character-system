# P6 Animation bake-off (M1, T24)

**Question.** Which free source gives the best rotation-only clips on *our* skeleton (MPFB
`game_engine`, canonical rest = MakeHuman default body), and can the retarget be a committed,
re-runnable script?

**Answer.** Yes, it scripts end to end (`pipeline/blender/m1_anim/run_all.sh`, deterministic
bit-for-bit). No single source wins every clip:

| clip | winner | runner-up | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `idle` | **CMU 113_21** (female, "Standing Still") | Mesh2Motion `Idle_Subtle` | the only relaxed, natural stand; both Mesh2Motion idles are bent-knee "ready" stances with fists, KayKit holds its arms out wide |
| `walk` | **CMU 113_25** | Mesh2Motion `Walk_Female` (close) | natural female gait with real hip sway and soft arm swing; `Walk_Female` is clean but stylized; Mesh2Motion `Walk` leans forward with fists; KayKit is a high-knee prance |
| `sit` | **Mesh2Motion `Sitting_Idle`** | CMU 113_15 | calm, hands resting on knees (a contact pose), clean loop; CMU's hands hover and its seated stretch is only 1 s |
| `sit_down` | **Mesh2Motion `Sitting_Enter`** | CMU 113_15 | clean bend-and-lower that we end exactly on `sit` frame 0; CMU's take starts mid-bend and has a 180 deg hand flip; KayKit starts kneeling |
| `arms_up` | **CMU 113_23** ("Stretch and yawn") | KayKit `Cheer` | full overhead reach and back down, the stress pose P4 needs; KayKit only reaches a V, Mesh2Motion only has jumping jacks |
| `wave` | **CMU 113_27** | Mesh2Motion `Greeting` (close) | open-palm "hi" beside the head reads instantly; `Greeting` is a hand-on-hip finger wave (sassier, smaller) |
| `run` (opt.) | **Mesh2Motion `Run_Female`** | CMU 113_07 | athletic run with a flight phase; CMU's take is a slow jog |
| `crouch` (opt.) | Mesh2Motion `Crouch_Idle` | (only candidate) | |
| `arms_crossed` (opt.) | Mesh2Motion `Idle_FoldArms` | (only candidate) | needs a 10 deg arm offset to clear the default bust |

**KayKit loses every clip.** Its rig is chibi (hips 0.41 m, big head, short legs), and its
animations are posed for those proportions: arms held 45-90 deg out to clear the head and body,
knees lifted to hip height when walking, legs thrown forward when seated. Rotation transfer
copies that faithfully, so on a realistic body it reads as cartoony. Licence is clean (CC0); it
could still serve stylized emotes.

No bone flips: the largest frame-to-frame rotation in any exported clip is real motion (a
running thigh, a waving forearm, a toe rolling off the floor); quaternion signs are kept
continuous. All per-clip numbers: `metrics.json`.

**Stride (walk, neutral body):** 1.306 m per 1.267 s cycle (two steps of 0.65 m) = 1.03 m/s at
1x playback, from the planted foot's backward speed (the source pelvis travelled 1.27 m per
cycle scaled onto our legs, 3 % below the foot-based figure). `run`: 2.31 m per 0.833 s =
2.77 m/s.

**Seat (sit):** seat surface 0.415 m above the floor (lowest point of the thigh/buttock
underside on the skinned default body), buttock contact 0.30-0.40 m behind the root, hip joints
0.38 m behind, pelvis head 0.509 m high; feet stay where the standing feet are.

**Checks.** `30_export.py` compares every joint's rest TRS in the GLB with
`canonical_rest.json`: max 0.0001 deg / 1 um. `check_glb.mjs` loads the GLB in Babylon 9.30
(NullEngine): 53 bones, 9 groups, each with 53 rotation channels + 1 translation (pelvis), every
target a canonical bone, 30 fps; then plays every group and compares six joint heights at two
frames with the Blender FK: max error 0.006 mm.

## Method (details: `pipeline/blender/m1_anim/README.md`)

World-space rotation transfer with rest alignment: our bone's world rotation =
(source joint's world rotation away from its own rest) x (alignment) x (our rest). Alignment is
identity for torso/head/clavicles (both rests are "standing upright"), a swing of the bone
direction for limbs (their T-pose vs our A-pose), and swing + twist from the across-palm vector
for forearm and hand (`alignment.jpg`). Fingers without data get a relaxed curl. Clean-up:
in-place locomotion, feet anchored under the root, loop closure (best window by joint rotations
and limb velocities, then the residual spread linearly), then two-bone leg IK pinning planted
feet with each sole on the floor, toe roll at toe-off, and CMU head damping + levelling.

Lessons on the way (now encoded in the pipeline):
- **CMU frame rates differ from the site's label.** Subject 141's AMCs are 60 fps although listed
  at 120 (its walk would be 220 steps/min); subject 113 really is 120. Measured from cadence.
- **CMU 113's head channel is unreliable** (50-100 deg against the thorax while "standing
  still"); we keep 20-30 % of its motion and re-level the gaze.
- **Mesh2Motion's addon clips are cyclic without a duplicated end key**: sampled as wrapping
  (raw residual 8-17 deg -> 0).
- **Close the loop before IK**: spreading a residual after IK un-pinned the feet (1.3 cm slide).
- **Match loop windows by rotations, not positions**: a position match picked a CMU window
  where the walker was still accelerating (35 deg foot residual).
- **A rigid toe sets the ground at toe-off**: grounding on the lowest toe point made the stance
  foot float 27 mm. Ground on heel/ball, then bend the toes.

## Licences and doubts

- **CMU** (`mocap.cs.cmu.edu` home page): "free for all uses ... You may include this data in
  commercially-sold products, but you may not resell this data directly, even in converted
  form." Shipping retargeted clips inside the game is including them in a product; selling or
  separately distributing a clip pack would not be. The GLB is downloadable by players as part
  of the game, which I read as allowed; a credit line ("motion data from mocap.cs.cmu.edu,
  funded by NSF EIA-0196217") is requested and cheap. **Flag for the user.**
- **Mesh2Motion**: the repository dedicates "all 3d models, blend files, rigs, animations" to
  CC0 (`LICENSE-CC0.MD`), but individual clips carry no provenance. We did not use
  `human-mocap-animations.glb` (its CMU sample folder points at a third-party itch.io pack of
  unclear licence). Low risk, noted.
- **KayKit** Adventurers 1.0: CC0 (`LICENSE.txt`); not exported. The 133-clip "KayKit Character
  Animations" pack named in PLAN R10 is itch.io-only; I used the same author's GitHub pack
  (75 clips, same chibi rig family) for a scriptable download.
- Not touched: Quaternius, Mixamo, LAFAN1, AMASS, Bandai Namco.

## Known defects (look at these at the M1 gate)

1. **CMU head motion is mostly synthetic.** 113's head data is unusable as captured; the idle
   keeps 20 % of it, wave/arms_up 30 %, re-levelled. In a few idle frames the head still tilts a
   few degrees.
2. **Idle posture** is a real relaxed stand: soft knees, slightly rounded shoulders, hands
   hanging just in front of the thighs (right fingertips touch the thigh). Some may read it as
   slouchy.
3. **Walk** (CMU): feet land close to the midline (a slight cross-over line, natural for this
   subject); toes bend up to 46 deg at toe-off; the 7.5 deg forearm residual is spread over the
   cycle.
4. **Wave** is one 0.6 s oscillation, so it repeats fast; the forearm moves 41 deg/frame (real
   speed, may strobe at low frame rates); 11.9 deg forearm residual spread; the subject's right
   ankle sits ~10 cm above the left throughout the take (foot on a step?), so the legs are a synthetic hip-width stance under her
   pelvis.
5. **sit_down** starts from a wide, fisted stance (feet pinned where the seated feet go) and
   bends far forward mid-way; it needs a crossfade from `idle`.
6. **sit**: strong forward lean, fingers rest on the kneecaps (touching).
7. **arms_up** starts with the hands at the chest (the source take), not from a neutral stand,
   and arches the lower back strongly overhead. 7.6 s, the biggest clip.
8. **arms_crossed**: forearms still touch the default bust after the 10 deg forward offset;
   wider bodies need D10 contact IK.
9. **Hands** on KayKit/CMU clips use a relaxed-curl preset (no finger data); Mesh2Motion clips
   carry the source's own (often fisted) fingers.
10. Only the neutral body was rendered. Envelope bodies, D10 corrections (arm splay, stride
    scaling) and garments are later work (T8, T26).
